\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Cooperative Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) has achieved notable empirical success across symmetric coordination tasks, most prominently in the StarCraft Multi-Agent Challenge (SMAC)~\cite{yu2022surprising,rashid2020monotonic} and cooperative swarm robotics. In these domains, agents typically share homogeneous action sets, symmetric observation fields, and interchangeable tactical objectives. However, many practical multi-agent settings---such as search-and-rescue, multi-robot warehouse logistics, and security response---require \emph{heterogeneous specialization}: agents possess asymmetric sensory capabilities, distinct action mechanics, and non-interchangeable functional responsibilities.

Under asymmetric role allocations, standard Centralized Training with Decentralized Execution (CTDE) methods~\cite{schulman2017proximal,foerster2018counterfactual} encounter three fundamental bottlenecks:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
    \item \textbf{Monolithic Representation Bottlenecks:} Shared-parameter networks struggle to represent diverse, role-specific policies when sub-task rewards arrive at differing temporal frequencies and reward densities.
    \item \textbf{Routing Imbalance in Value Bidding:} Unconstrained value-bidding Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models~\cite{shazeer2017outrageously,fedus2022switch} frequently collapse into a single dominant expert that wins all routing decisions, preventing other experts from receiving training data.
    \item \textbf{Uncalibrated Initial Values:} Introducing a new expert to address specific sub-task failures is ineffective if its uncalibrated value network cannot submit accurate bids against mature generalists in global routing.
\end{enumerate}

To address these challenges, we examine an architectural progression across four iterative configurations, culminating in our proposed framework:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
    \item \textbf{MARC (Multi-Agent Reward Credit):} Monolithic CTDE with potential-based reward shaping to assist spatial credit assignment.
    \item \textbf{COOP (Cooperative MoE):} A static 2-expert value-bidding pool, showing the utility of modular capacity while experiencing routing imbalance.
    \item \textbf{E-COOP (Evolutionary Cooperative PPO):} An evolutionary baseline introducing periodic Fisher-weighted parameter recombination into a consolidated policy ($K=1$). While sample-efficient, parameter consolidation can overwrite specialized sub-policies.
    \item \textbf{THIEF:} Our complete framework, combining plateau-triggered expert spawning, failure-directed gradient updates, Fisher-weighted parameter recombination across experts, isolated warmup on dedicated environment shards, and load-balancing regularization with inactive expert pruning.
\end{enumerate}

We introduce the open-source \textbf{HEIST Benchmark} (implemented as a native multi-threaded Rust engine sustaining approximately $12,\!000$ environment steps per second) and evaluate our iterative method lineage against standard external MARL baselines (MAPPO~\cite{yu2022surprising}, H-MAPPO~\cite{vezhnevets2017feudal}, and COMA~\cite{foerster2018counterfactual}) across a 5-stage spatial curriculum ($11 \times 11$ to $50 \times 50$).

In summary, this paper makes the following contributions:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
    \item \textbf{HEIST Benchmark:} an open-source, high-throughput heterogeneous cooperative MARL environment with asymmetric role mechanics, dynamic adversarial security, and a five-stage spatial curriculum, engineered in Rust with zero-copy PyTorch integration for GPU-accelerated training.
    \item \textbf{THIEF:} a dynamic Mixture-of-Experts algorithm that triggers expert spawning on detected win-rate plateaus, recombines parameters weighted by Fisher Information, warms up new experts on dedicated environment shards, and prevents routing imbalance via hysteresis-bounded bidding and load-balancing regularization.
    \item \textbf{A systematic method lineage as macro-ablation:} MARC, COOP, and E-COOP isolate, in controlled succession, the contributions of credit shaping, modular capacity, and evolutionary recombination, and are benchmarked against external baselines (MAPPO, H-MAPPO, COMA) over $3,\!000$ evaluation episodes per stage.
\end{enumerate}
