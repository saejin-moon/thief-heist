Heterogeneous cooperative Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) in partially observable, risk-sensitive environments presents severe challenges for decentralized policy synthesis and asymmetric skill acquisition. Standard Centralized Training with Decentralized Execution (CTDE) frameworks suffer from representation collapse when mapping disparate operational roles into monolithic parameter spaces, while unconstrained Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures degenerate into monopoly regimes that starve emerging specialists of training transitions. In this work, we introduce \textbf{HEIST}, an open-source, high-throughput cooperative benchmark featuring four asymmetric tactical roles---Scout, Hacker, Muscle, and Extractor---with raytraced field-of-view perception, dynamic adversarial patrols, and spatial long-horizon escape mechanics, implemented as a native multi-threaded Rust engine sustaining approximately $12,\!000$ environment steps per second. To resolve coordination failures across expanding spatial scales, we present a systematic iterative method lineage progressing through four algorithmic stages: \textbf{MARC} (causal credit shaping), \textbf{COOP} (static value-bidding MoE), \textbf{E-COOP} (evolutionary parameter crossover with critic cloning), and culminating in \textbf{THIEF} (\textbf{T}argeted \textbf{H}ysteresis-routed \textbf{I}ncubated \textbf{E}volution via \textbf{F}isher-geometry). THIEF detects empirical win-rate stagnation to spawn specialist policies directed along failure sub-manifolds, recombines multi-parent parameter manifolds via all-pool Fisher Information geometry, incubates new specialists in dedicated environment shards to eliminate cold-start critic fragility, and enforces sparse MoE load-balancing regularization alongside dynamic dormancy freezing. Across an extensive five-stage spatial curriculum ($11 \times 11$ to $50 \times 50$) evaluated over 1,000 independent episodes per seed across 3 random seeds, we demonstrate that each architectural iteration systematically unlocks higher-order spatial coordination: on the largest $50 \times 50$ stage, THIEF and E-COOP outperform the strongest external baseline (MAPPO) by more than $50\%$ in relative escape success, and their advantage over MAPPO on Stage~2 is statistically significant (two-sided Welch's $t$-test, $p < 0.01$).
