Cooperative Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) in partially observable environments requires coordinating heterogeneous agents with asymmetric physical capabilities, distinct sensory perimeters, and sequential subtask dependencies. In such domains, standard Centralized Training with Decentralized Execution (CTDE) frameworks suffer from representation collapse: dense early-reward subtasks generate dominant gradient updates that starve and destabilize sparse, late-reward objectives. Conversely, unconstrained Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures frequently succumb to routing monopoly, where an initial dominant generalist captures all routing while newly introduced specialists remain uncalibrated. We present \textbf{THIEF} (\emph{Targeted Heterogeneous Isolated Evolution with Fisher-guided recombination}), a dynamic MoE framework operating under an orchestrated \emph{Centralized Dispatch with Decentralized Execution} (CDDE) paradigm. THIEF detects gradient interference on empirical win-rate plateaus, isolates failure transitions, and spawns targeted specialist policies. To initialize specialists stably, THIEF adapts Fisher-weighted model merging as an online Riemannian recombination operator and pre-trains candidates on isolated environment shards to calibrate critic estimates before global dispatch. During execution, a centralized dispatcher assigns specialists under a state-dependent hysteresis barrier that prevents high-frequency thrashing, while load-balancing entropy regularization averts single-expert monopoly and policy actors execute strictly decentralized on local egocentric observations. Benchmarked on \textbf{HEIST}---a high-throughput, multi-threaded Rust simulation engine ($\sim 12,\!000$ steps/sec) across four asymmetric roles over a 5-stage spatial curriculum ($N=450,\!000$ evaluation episodes across 10 random seeds)---THIEF consistently outperforms eight competitive baselines including ROMA, RODE, and MAPPO. On the hardest $50 \times 50$ facility (Stage 4), THIEF achieves a \textbf{13.3\% $\pm$ 2.4\%} full-squad escape win rate, delivering a \textbf{+70.5\% relative gain} over the closest modular baseline (COOP at 7.8\%) and \textbf{+101.5\%} over monolithic MAPPO (6.6\%; Welch's $t$-test, $p < 10^{-3}$).
