\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Cooperative Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) has achieved impressive milestones in symmetric benchmark environments, such as the StarCraft Multi-Agent Challenge (SMAC)~\cite{yu2022surprising,rashid2020monotonic} and cooperative navigation testbeds. In these classic formulations, agents are largely homogeneous: they share symmetric sensor suites, interchangeable actuation primitives, and concurrent objective structures. However, real-world multi-agent systems---including tactical search-and-rescue, multi-robot warehouse logistics, and security response---inherently demand \emph{heterogeneous specialization}. In these missions, agents operate with strictly asymmetric sensory perimeters, disparate physical capabilities, and asynchronous, sequential functional dependencies.

When applied to heterogeneous coordination under partial observability, standard Centralized Training with Decentralized Execution (CTDE) frameworks~\cite{foerster2018counterfactual,kuba2022trust} encounter three foundational bottlenecks:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
    \item \textbf{Temporal Reward-Density Mismatch \& Representation Interference:} Shared monolithic policy networks suffer catastrophic interference when heterogeneous roles exhibit divergent reward densities. High-frequency subtasks (e.g., local scout reconnaissance) generate dense, high-magnitude gradient signals that overwhelm and overwrite the sparse, delayed gradient signals required for intricate multi-step tasks (e.g., security terminal override and vault extraction).
    \item \textbf{Routing Collapse \& Monopoly Regimes:} Although Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures~\cite{shazeer2017outrageously,fedus2022switch} offer modular capacity, unconstrained scalar value-bidding mechanisms in RL quickly collapse into single-expert monopolies. A slightly superior initial generalist receives all routing assignments, starving alternative experts of exploratory experience and collapsing multi-expert models back into monolithic parameterizations.
    \item \textbf{The Cold-Start Value Calibration Deficit:} When a newly spawned specialist policy is introduced to resolve specific failure transitions, its uncalibrated critic network cannot submit accurate value bids against established generalists. In unconstrained global routing, the newcomer is permanently outbid and rendered functionally dormant before receiving gradient updates.
\end{enumerate}

To resolve these pathologies, we present a compositional systems approach rather than an isolated algorithmic heuristic. We introduce \textbf{THIEF} (\emph{Targeted Heterogeneous Isolated Evolution with Fisher-guided recombination}), an open-ended dynamic MoE framework tailored for heterogeneous multi-agent coordination. THIEF formulates policy capacity expansion as a closed-loop diagnostic cycle:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
    \item \emph{Deficit Detection:} THIEF monitors empirical team win rates over sliding episode windows, detecting performance plateaus and diagnosing multi-task gradient conflict between success and failure trajectories.
    \item \emph{Curvature-Optimal Recombination:} Rather than relying on random Gaussian perturbations or unconstrained multi-task surgery, THIEF synthesizes candidate specialists by recombining parent parameters weighted by their empirical Fisher Information matrices~\cite{matena2021merging}, followed by a targeted deficit policy-gradient update on isolated failure transitions.
    \item \emph{Sandbox Incubation:} Newly spawned specialists are assigned to isolated parallel simulation shards for a calibrated warmup period ($\tau_{\text{iso}}$ updates), allowing critic networks to anchor their value estimates before competing in global routing.
    \item \emph{Hysteresis Bounded Routing:} During execution, a state-dependent switching barrier prevents high-frequency thrashing between experts across consecutive steps, while auxiliary load-balancing entropy regularization prevents monopoly regimes.
\end{itemize}

We evaluate THIEF on \textbf{HEIST}, an open-source, high-throughput cooperative stealth benchmark engineered with a multi-threaded Rust Rayon simulation engine sustaining over $12,\!000$ environment steps per second with zero-copy PyTorch tensor sharding. To rigorously dissect the design space, we evaluate a controlled four-iteration development lineage as progressive macro-ablations: \textbf{MARC} (credit assignment via reward shaping), \textbf{COOP} (static 2-expert value bidding), \textbf{E-COOP} (evolutionary Fisher recombination with single-champion consolidation), and \textbf{THIEF} (full dynamic deficit-targeted MoE). We benchmark this lineage against leading external MARL algorithms---including MAPPO~\cite{yu2022surprising}, H-MAPPO (our FeUdal-Networks-style~\cite{vezhnevets2017feudal} adaptation of MAPPO~\cite{yu2022surprising}), COMA~\cite{foerster2018counterfactual}, ROMA~\cite{wang2020roma}, and RODE~\cite{wang2021rode}---across a five-stage spatial curriculum ($11 \times 11$ to $50 \times 50$) over \textbf{10 independent training seeds} with \textbf{1,000 stochastic evaluation episodes per checkpoint} ($N=450,\!000$ total evaluation episodes across all nine algorithms under the identical protocol).

Our primary contributions are summarized as follows:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
    \item \textbf{The HEIST Benchmark:} A high-throughput, procedurally generated Dec-POMDP benchmark featuring four asymmetric roles (Scout, Hacker, Muscle, Extractor), dynamic adversarial guard patrols with raytraced line-of-sight perception, and a multi-stage curriculum.
    \item \textbf{The THIEF Framework:} A principled composition combining plateau-triggered specialist spawning, curvature-optimal Fisher parameter recombination, isolated sandbox incubation, and hysteresis-bounded load-balanced routing.
    \item \textbf{Empirical Scalability \& Rigor:} Extensive benchmarking across 450 evaluated checkpoints demonstrating that THIEF delivers a \textbf{+70.5\% relative win rate advantage} over the strongest monolithic baseline on the hardest $50 \times 50$ stage ($13.3\% \pm 2.4\%$ vs.\ $7.8\% \pm 1.7\%$ for COOP and $7.6\% \pm 1.4\%$ for E-COOP), and outperforms role-oriented baselines ROMA ($9.4\% \pm 1.2\%$) and RODE ($5.2\% \pm 1.0\%$); two-sample Welch's $t$-test, $p < 10^{-3}$ across all baselines.
\end{enumerate}
