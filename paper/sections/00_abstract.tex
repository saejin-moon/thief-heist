Heterogeneous cooperative Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) in partially observable environments requires coordinating agents with asymmetric capabilities and sequential task dependencies. Standard Centralized Training with Decentralized Execution (CTDE) approaches struggle to represent diverse specialized behaviors in a single policy, while standard Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures often suffer from routing imbalance where a single expert dominates training. In this work, we introduce \textbf{HEIST}, an open-source, high-throughput benchmark featuring four asymmetric roles---Scout, Hacker, Muscle, and Extractor---with line-of-sight perception, patrolling adversaries, and multi-stage extraction objectives, supported by a multi-threaded Rust simulation engine (~$12,\!000$ steps/sec). We evaluate several architectural variants: \textbf{MARC} (reward credit shaping), \textbf{COOP} (static value-bidding MoE), \textbf{E-COOP} (value-bidding MoE with parameter recombination), and \textbf{THIEF} (value-bidding MoE with error-driven expert spawning, Fisher-weighted parameter recombination, isolated warmup environments, and routing hysteresis). THIEF detects win-rate plateaus to spawn new expert policies targeted at failure cases, recombines parent parameters weighted by value estimates and Fisher information, warms up new experts on isolated environment shards to calibrate value estimates, and applies load-balancing regularization with inactive expert pruning. Across a five-stage spatial curriculum ($11 \times 11$ to $50 \times 50$) evaluated over 1,000 episodes per seed across 3 random seeds, dynamic expert models outperform monolithic baselines: on the $50 \times 50$ stage, THIEF and E-COOP achieve over $50\%$ higher relative escape success compared to MAPPO, with a statistically significant advantage on Stage~2 (Welch's $t$-test, $p < 0.01$).
