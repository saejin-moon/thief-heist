\section{Discussion \& Ablation Analysis}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Architectural Lineage as Progressive Macro-Ablations.}
The progression across our four development iterations provides a rigorous, step-wise ablation of the architectural decisions underlying THIEF:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
    \item \textbf{Monolithic vs.\ Modular Capacity (MARC vs.\ COOP):} Moving from a single monolithic network with reward credit shaping (MARC) to a 2-expert value-bidding architecture (COOP) improves Stage 2 win rate from $72.2\%$ to $75.4\%$. However, COOP demonstrates the ``rich-get-richer'' pathology in its purest form: routing telemetry shows a single expert capturing effectively $100\%$ of dispatch on Stages 2--3 across all three seeds, reducing the MoE to a monolithic policy with redundant capacity.
    \item \textbf{Static vs.\ Evolutionary Adaptation (COOP vs.\ E-COOP):} Incorporating Fisher Information parameter recombination in E-COOP boosts win rate to $80.8\%$ on Stage 2 and $37.2\%$ on Stage 3. Yet E-COOP implements \emph{critic cloning} targeting a single elite champion ($K=1$). The newly spawned mutant aggressively cannibalizes the parent network, collapsing diverse sub-skills back into a monolithic manifold.
    \item \textbf{Aggressive Cannibalization vs.\ Incubated Specialization (E-COOP vs.\ THIEF):} E-COOP serves as the primary macro-architectural ablation of THIEF: it preserves Fisher recombination but ablates sandbox incubation, dynamic plateau triggering, and load-balancing regularization. While E-COOP achieves high sample efficiency on Stage 3, THIEF prevents monopoly collapse, allocating disparate operational duties across distinct specialists while matching E-COOP on Stage 4 escape ($12.9\%$ vs.\ $13.8\%$).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Emergent Routing Telemetry of THIEF.}
Training logs provide direct evidence of how the THIEF routing dynamics behave in practice. Plateau-triggered spawning activates reliably across seeds: the specialist pool grows from a single generalist to $K = 5$ active experts by Stage 2 and $K = 8$ by Stages 3--4 (7 total spawning events per seed). The hysteresis barrier suppresses high-frequency expert flapping as designed---per-episode expert switch rates remain below $1\%$ at all stages (typically $0.1$--$0.6\%$)---while dispatch telemetry shows the pool differentiating into a small set of heavily utilized specialists alongside dormant candidates that are subsequently frozen by the dormancy mechanism. This confirms that value-bidding routing with hysteresis does not degenerate into per-step policy oscillation, and that load-balancing regularization keeps secondary experts eligible for dispatch without penalizing task performance. Controlled component-level ablations of each mechanism (incubation, Fisher geometry, load balancing, hysteresis, and the plateau trigger) are reported in Appendix~\ref{sec:app_ablation}.

\paragraph{Computational Complexity \& Scalability.}
THIEF introduces negligible computational overhead over standard PPO. The per-expert Fisher Information Matrix is maintained as a diagonal running average requiring $\mathcal{O}(D)$ memory per expert, where $D \approx 1.8 \times 10^{5}$ parameters per specialist (actor and centralized critic). Parameter recombination and deficit mutation execute solely upon plateau events, incurring an $\mathcal{O}(KD)$ parameter update ($K \le 8$ observed in all runs) that completes in well under a second on GPU, accounting for less than $0.2\%$ of total training wall-clock time.

\paragraph{Limitations and Future Work.}
While THIEF significantly improves specialization and end-stage success, routing on ultra-large grids ($50 \times 50$) remains constrained by the finite receptive field ($7 \times 7$) of egocentric observations. Additionally, our statistical comparisons rely on three independent training seeds: the Stage 2 advantage over MAPPO is significant (Welch's $p < 0.01$), but higher-stage comparisons would benefit from additional seeds given the large per-stage variance in the long-horizon regime. Future extensions will investigate graph neural network communication backbones and self-supervised spatial goal abstraction.
